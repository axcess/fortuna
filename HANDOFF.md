# Handoff: The Guildmaster's Desk

A handoff from one long working session, written to pick the project up in a new chat. Its first use is exploring whether the idea could become a **RimWorld mod**.

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **The game:** a browser game (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step) called *The Guildmaster's Desk*. You run a run-down adventurers' guild in a fantasy town, but you never fight. You're middle management: you read letters, work out what clients aren't telling you, post jobs, approve expenses, keep the Council happy and pass the weekly inspection. Your members decide overnight which jobs to take, and how a job goes depends on how well you classified it.
- **The art:** painted pixel-art rooms you point and click through, plus 70-odd painted character sprites (12 adventurer classes, staff and familiars). All of it was generated with Nano Banana and cut into sprites by a Python tool in the repo.
- **What's next:** explore turning it into a RimWorld mod. Section 9 has a first-pass analysis of what fits, what doesn't and a suggested first prototype.

## 3. How the project evolved

1. **Kairosoft-style tycoon:** the first ask was a game like *Dino Park Tycoon* (MECC, 1995) and Kairosoft games, about restoring a dilapidated adventurers' guild into a powerhouse, Stardew-farm style. Setting: WoW / D&D 5e.
2. **Deeper simulation:** the user asked for SimCity 3/4-style simulation of individual characters. This became the real-time **Guildhall** sim, with needs, moods, social life and breakdowns. It still exists on `claude/tender-clarke-9qzql4`.
3. **The pivot:** "choices matter", seen from the Guild Leader's desk, point and click.
4. **Paper-pusher version:** the user explicitly wanted a *boring paper pusher / middle management* feel: morning coffee, daily events, requests from nobles, townsfolk and the council, posting quests, hiring and training staff, running the tavern, inn, training yard and academy, and walking round the hall talking to adventurers. This became **The Guildmaster's Desk**, on its own branch.
5. **Visual upgrades:**
   - floating labels on clickable zones;
   - action-cost floats;
   - speech bubbles;
   - painted room backgrounds (Z-Image in ComfyUI first, then Nano Banana 2.1);
   - the internal resolution doubled to 640×360;
   - painted sprites for every character.
6. **Classes:** the cast grew from 5 classes to 12, each with a rule that changes how you do paperwork.

## 4. Core design of the desk game

**Design pillar:** you are the manager, never the hero. The fun is in reading carefully, reading between the lines, and living with bureaucratic consequences.

### The working day
- **Time budget:** 08:00 to 18:00, with overtime allowed until 22:00. Everything costs minutes:
  - skim a letter: 3
  - read it carefully: 12+
  - reply: 5
  - draft a posting: 25
  - talk to someone: 10
  - interview an applicant: 20
  - walk to another room: 5
- **Focus:** drains as you work. Coffee sips and refills restore it.
- **Skimming vs reading:** skimming is cheap but shows only the summary. Reading carefully shows the clues. A hired clerk pins a helpful note to each letter.

### Requests and their hidden truth
- **What a request hides:** every request has a secret *truth*, made of a real rank (F to A), real hazards and what is actually out there. Hazard tags: beasts, undead, sorcery, poison/disease, fire, large monster, great numbers, discretion, night work, water, escort duty.
- **Clues:** letters carry clue sentences tied to hazards. Clients understate: nobles hide things, and farmers exaggerate or panic.
- **Your options:**
  - ask for more detail (costs goodwill, delays the job, brings a follow-up letter);
  - decline;
  - post it on posting form QP-1, choosing rank, party size, pay per head per day and hazard tags.

### Overnight claiming
- **Who takes what:** members choose jobs themselves. Interest is pay compared with what they expect (`EXPECT[rank] × (1 + 0.2 × how overqualified they are)`).
- **Traits shift interest:** greedy, modest, brave and cautious members weigh jobs differently. Members also prefer jobs whose hazards their class is good against.
- **Lapsing:** a posting nobody claims for 3 nights lapses, and the client is angry.

### Resolution
- **The odds:** party power (from rank, experience, morale and the brave trait) is set against the true threat. Each hazard you tagged gives +6%. Each one you missed gives −18%, and you're told about it. A class that's good against a hazard gives +12%.
- **Outcomes:** a success, great success, failure or disaster decides injuries and deaths. A cleric in the party cuts both.
- **What comes back:** a report document, a thank-you or angry letter from the client, an expense claim, and sometimes Form 9.
- **Misclassification inquiry:** if a job fails *and* you got the rank or hazards wrong, the Council fines you 30 gold. This keeps lazy classification from being the best strategy.

### Paperwork systems
- **Expense claims:** you tick lines to approve. Members with the *padder* trait slip in fake lines, and you learn to spot them, which reveals the trait.
- **Invoices:** charter fee, ale, laundry, roofers and so on. You can pay them or dispute them. Unpaid ones bring final notices.
- **Council directives arrive over time:**
  - the Hazard Disclosure Act;
  - Form 9 (disposal of monster remains);
  - the Nocturnal Operations Permit;
  - the quarterly membership census.
- **Weekly inspection:** graded on paperwork, success rate and classification accuracy.
- **Other mail:** promotion petitions (liars over-claim), applications (interview them to learn traits), resignations, death benefits, complaints about rowdy members, builders' proposals and the town gazette, which carries rumours and dungeon leads.

### Guild progression
- **Renown** moves the guild through ranks: Provisional Charter (F), Registered (E), Licensed (D), Chartered (C), Royal Charter (B), Guild of the Realm (A).
- **Facilities:** tavern levels 1–3, inn levels 1–4 (bed capacity 4/7/10/14, which caps the roster), training yard levels 1–3, infirmary, archive and Academy (needs rank D).
- **Staff:**
  - clerk (summary notes on letters);
  - barkeep (tavern income and rumours);
  - drill sergeant (double training experience);
  - innkeeper (happier members);
  - dean (runs the Academy).
- **Traits** (hidden until revealed): honest or padder, brave or cautious, tidy or rowdy, greedy or modest, and liar.

### Classes (12)

| Class | Good against | Special rule |
|---|---|---|
| Warrior | large monsters, great numbers, escort | none |
| Ranger | beasts, night work, water | none |
| Cleric | undead, poison | Fewer injuries and deaths for the whole party |
| Mage | sorcery, fire | Learns fastest at the Academy |
| Rogue | discretion, night work | none |
| Paladin | undead, escort | none |
| Summoner | sorcery, great numbers | Keeps a familiar (golem, whelp, wisp, fox, raven or lantern-spirit) drawn beside them |
| Bard | escort, discretion | Writes the party's report as a ballad, and the summary hides the outcome. Busks in the tavern for renown and gold when idle |
| Alchemist | poison, fire | Real reagent lines on expense claims, so big bills aren't always padding. Speeds infirmary healing when idle |
| Monk | great numbers, discretion | Always honest and modest. Takes any job and draws no pay, but the Abbey sends a weekly tithe invoice |
| Hedge-witch | sorcery, night work | Needs a Council licence (15 gold for 4 weeks). If unlicensed on a job, a 50% chance of a 20-gold fine. Sometimes pencils a hidden-hazard clue into the margin of new requests |
| Artificer | large monsters, water | While one is idle, building works cost 25% less and finish in a day |
| Druid | beasts, poison | The party isn't penalised for unlisted beasts or water |

Mage, cleric, summoner, witch and monk learn fastest at the Academy.

## 5. Rooms and presentation

- **Rooms:** office (the desk), hall, tavern, training yard, inn (with an infirmary variant) and academy. Each has a painted background (`art/<room>.png`, 640×360) and an `alt` layout in `scenes.js` with clickable zones fitted to the painting.
- **Dynamic effects over the paintings:** time of day (window tint, darkness, lamp glow), fire flicker, motes, birds, and upgrade variants (`yard_2`, `yard_3`, `inn_infirmary`).
- **People:** sprites stand at fixed spots in each room. You click someone to talk to them, and ambient speech bubbles pop up.
- **Labels:** floating tags mark exits, people and things. The L key toggles them, and they're on by default on touch screens.

## 6. Art pipeline (what worked)

### Backgrounds
- The originals are about 1376×768 and live in `art/source/`. They're shrunk to 640×360 with box resampling.
- **Generation:** generate with Nano Banana 2.1, using an earlier room as the style reference.

### Character sprites
1. **Lock-in message:** start a fresh Nano chat with two reference images (the warrior class sheet and the starter lineup) and a lock-in message covering:
   - chunky pixel art with thick dark outlines;
   - flat 2–3 tone shading, with no glow or blur;
   - a front-facing neutral pose with feet on one baseline;
   - a flat **#FF00FF** background with gaps between figures;
   - "the references show STYLE and SCALE only: never reuse their faces, hair, helmets or outfits".
2. **One sheet per message:** four figures, each fully described (sex, age, build, skin, hair, face, outfit), with hands empty at the sides. Without full descriptions Nano makes everyone male and copies faces from the reference.
3. **Cutting:** `python3 tools/cut_sprites.py SHEET.jpg name1 name2 ...` writes the sprites, portraits and `js/sprites.js`. The full command list is in `art/README.md`. The tool:
   - keys out the magenta and strips the magenta fringe;
   - splits figures by connected shape, and falls back to seams along outlines when figures touch;
   - uses one global scale, so a human is 56 px, which is 28 game units;
   - crisps the alpha, finds a foot anchor and makes a 96×96 head portrait.
4. **Naming:**
   - `<class>_<variant>` (for example `warrior_orc`) goes into class pools automatically. A member's look is picked from their id, so it's stable.
   - Named characters are mapped by full name in `Scenes.SPRITE_OF`.
   - Staff are `staff_*`, mapped by fixed ids.
   - Familiars are `fam_*`.

**Have so far:** 4 starters; 4 looks each for warrior, mage, rogue, paladin, cleric, bard, alchemist, monk, witch, artificer, druid and ranger, plus 8 for summoner; 5 staff; 6 familiars.

## 7. Open items in the desk game

- **Seated poses:** seated characters are a hip-height crop of the standing sprite. A seated sheet would fix this.
- **Sergeant's hands:** her hands were hidden behind her neighbours on the staff sheet. A solo re-render would fix it.
- **Second sheets:** a second sheet per class would give 8 looks each. Monks are the most lopsided at 3 men and 1 woman.
- **Names don't fit race:** you can get an orc called "Elowen". Race is already in each sprite name, so this would be easy to tie up.
- **Untested path:** the unlicensed-witch fine was never triggered by the test bots, which always pay the licence.
- **Designed but not built: custom quests.** The player would write their own postings from city needs, rumours, region and NPC requesters. This was designed in conversation but never coded.

## 8. Testing approach used

- **Headless simulation bots:** Node `vm`, loading `content.js` and `core.js`, playing 28-day games in "careful" and "lazy" modes to check balance. The lazy bot must do worse.
- **Playwright scripts:** walking through the UI, screenshotting every room and checking for page errors.
- These lived in the session's scratchpad and are **not in the repo**. Recreate them if needed.

## 9. Exploring a RimWorld mod

> **Accuracy note:** the RimWorld points below are first-pass background knowledge, not checked against the current game. Check the current RimWorld version (the 1.5 to 1.6 era as of this writing), the modding docs and the decompiled source before committing to anything.

### Why it might fit
RimWorld already does what the old *Guildhall* sim was trying to do: deep simulation of individual pawns, with needs, moods, traits, relationships, injuries and mental breaks. What RimWorld doesn't have is the **desk layer**: requests with hidden truths, classification you can get wrong, expense fraud, bureaucracy and a manager who delegates instead of micromanaging. A mod could add that layer on top of a colony that *is* the guild.

### Concept mapping (desk game → RimWorld)

| Desk game | RimWorld counterpart / idea |
|---|---|
| The guild hall | The player's colony; the rooms already exist (tavern = rec room, inn = bedrooms, yard = training spots, infirmary = hospital) |
| Members (adventurers) | Colonists, or a separate "guild member" status for some of them |
| The desk / inbox | A new building, a "Guildmaster's Desk", that opens a custom window. Letters could also use RimWorld's letter stack |
| Request letters with hidden truth | Custom quests or incidents with a hidden real threat; the letter shows only clues |
| Posting form QP-1 | A custom window: choose rank, party size, pay and hazard tags |
| Members claim jobs themselves | Pawns volunteer based on traits, skills and pay; the player assigns no one directly (the core twist) |
| Party leaves on a job, returns days later | Pawns go off-map for N days, either abstractly or as a caravan or world object. Royalty seems to have quests where colonists are lent away and come back, worth studying as a template |
| Quest resolution (power vs threat) | Off-map resolution using pawn skills, health, gear and mood, then injuries applied as real hediffs on return |
| Reports, expense claims, invoices | Documents in the desk window, with padding tied to traits |
| Traits (padder, liar, greedy…) | Custom TraitDefs, or a "known/unknown" layer on top of existing traits |
| Council, inspection, directives | A faction-like "Town Council" with goodwill, fines and periodic inspections |
| Renown and guild rank F–A | A guild reputation track; Royalty's titles and permits may be a useful pattern |
| Classes | Pawn kinds, backstories and skill profiles. Class rules could become pawn abilities or simple modifiers |
| Staff (clerk, barkeep…) | Work assignments or custom roles with passive effects (Ideology roles may be a pattern) |

### Technical shape of a RimWorld mod (to verify)
- **Content:** XML Defs (`ThingDef`, `TraitDef`, `IncidentDef`, quest scripts and so on) for data. Most of `content.js` would become XML.
- **Logic:** a C# assembly targeting the .NET Framework version RimWorld uses, with **Harmony** patches where needed. Global state would live in a `GameComponent` or `WorldComponent` (the equivalent of `S`). The rules in `core.js` would port to C#.
- **UI:** custom windows built on RimWorld's immediate-mode UI (`Window` subclasses). The inbox, posting form, ledger and roster would be rebuilt there.
- **Art:** RimWorld pawns are layered textures (body, head, hair, apparel) seen top-down, so **the full-body front-facing sprites won't drop in as pawns.**
  - What could carry over: the portraits (UI and letters), the room paintings (as window backgrounds for the desk UI) and the document styling.
  - What would be new art: the desk building texture, and maybe apparel or class items.
- **Saving:** RimWorld's `Scribe` system, through `ExposeData`.

### Suggested first prototype
1. **A desk building:** a "Guildmaster's Desk" that opens an Inbox window.
2. **Generated requests:** a daily request generator using the same data (clients, problems, clues, hidden truth), ported from `content.js` with only 3–4 hazard types to start.
3. **A posting window:** set rank, size, pay and tags.
4. **Volunteering:** each evening, eligible colonists volunteer using a simplified interest formula based on skills and traits.
5. **Off-map jobs:** volunteers leave for the job's length (start abstract: despawn and hold them, then return them), resolved with the power vs threat model. On return they get injuries and a report document, and silver or goodwill is paid.
6. **Expense claims:** add one paperwork twist, padding by a "creative accountant" trait, to test whether the desk layer is fun inside RimWorld.

### Open questions to decide early
- **Who is the player?** Is the colony the guild, or does the guild sit beside an ordinary colony (a "guild charter" storyline)?
- **How far does it go?** A small add-on mod, or a big overhaul (the fantasy setting would clash with vanilla's sci-fi; fantasy/medieval mods exist and may pair well)?
- **Away time:** should pawns off on jobs be simulated as caravans or kept abstract? Abstract is far simpler and closer to the desk fantasy.
- **Does the desk fantasy survive?** RimWorld players are used to direct control, and "you can't assign people, only post jobs" may need an opt-in.
- **DLC:** which DLC to depend on, if any (Royalty and Ideology have systems that overlap).

### Reusable pieces from this project
- The **data tables** in `js/content.js`: problems, clues, client voices, expenses, complaints, directives and chat. These are pure content and port easily.
- The **resolution math** in `Core.resolve` and the **claim interest formula** in `Core.endDay`.
- The **class rule ideas** (ballad reports, tithes, licences, margin clues, foreman discounts): each one is a small, self-contained mechanic.
- The **Nano Banana prompting method** (section 6) for any new art.
